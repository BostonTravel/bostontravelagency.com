Swan Boats, ducklings, Harvard Yard and Boston after dark

Family Favorites & Evening Tours

Boston is one of the easiest big cities in America to visit with kids. It is compact and walkable, history comes with costumes and cannon fire, and some of the best attractions are free. When the kids are in bed, or if you are traveling without them, the city has plenty to do after dark too.

The Public Garden, Swan Boats and Make Way for Ducklings

Next to Boston Common, the Public Garden opened in 1837 as the first public botanical garden in America. Its pedal-powered Swan Boats have been gliding around the lagoon since 1877, run by the same family for generations. They operate from spring into early fall.

Near the entrance you will find a mother duck and her eight ducklings in bronze, from Robert McCloskey's 1941 children's book Make Way for Ducklings, which is set right here. Kids climb all over them, and on holidays the ducks are often dressed for the occasion. In winter, the Frog Pond on the Common becomes an outdoor skating rink.

Museums and attractions kids love

New England Aquarium. A giant four-story ocean tank, penguins, sea turtles and harbor seals on the waterfront.

Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum. Kids can join a colonial town meeting, board a replica ship and throw tea into the harbor themselves.

Museum of Science. Hands-on exhibits, lightning shows and a planetarium, right on the Charles River.

Boston Children's Museum. Look for the giant Hood milk bottle out front.

Harvard and MIT. Founded in 1636, Harvard is the oldest university in the country. Older kids love touring Harvard Yard, and the nearby MIT campus is full of science and quirky art.

Boston after dark

As the sun goes down, the city changes. A sunset cruise lights up the skyline. Ghost tours lead you by lantern through old burying grounds and the darker corners of colonial history. Symphony Hall, home of the Boston Symphony Orchestra and the Boston Pops, is considered one of the finest concert halls in the world, and the Theatre District hosts touring Broadway shows.

For a quieter evening, walk the gas-lit streets of Beacon Hill. Acorn Street, a tiny cobblestone lane, may be the most photographed street in the city.

Local tips

  • Ride the hop-on hop-off trolley early in the day, then hop off where the kids want to linger.
  • Ghost tours are best for ages 10 and up.
  • The Swan Boats are seasonal, usually mid-April through mid-September.
  • Many museums offer timed tickets. Book ahead on weekends and school vacation weeks.
