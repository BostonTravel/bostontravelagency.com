Eat like a local

What to eat in Boston

Boston isn't just lobster and chowder anymore, but the classics are classics for a reason. Here is what we tell friends and family they have to eat while they're in town, from North End cannoli to the North Shore roast beef sandwich locals argue about, plus a few of the places everyone is talking about right now.

The Boston classics

The great North End rivalry

Cannoli in the North End

No trip to Boston is complete without a cannoli, and you'll hear about the great North End rivalry before you even get there.

Mike's Pastry on Hanover Street is the famous one. Look for the line out the door and the white boxes tied with string all over the neighborhood. It's lively, crowded and a classic Boston photo, so if you want the selfie with the box, Mike's is your place, and you'll love it.

Modern Pastry, just across the street, is the one locals are more likely to name as their favorite. Its cannoli are filled to order, so the shells stay crisp. Bova's Bakery on Salem Street is the neighborhood's old-school standby, famous for being open around the clock and perfect for a late-night cannoli after dinner.

Sadly, many of the old-school North End bakeries are gone. Locals still miss Maria's Pastry Shop on Cross Street, run for decades by Maria Merola until she retired in 2019, and many of us remember it as the freshest and most authentic of them all. The good news is you won't be disappointed by any of the ones still standing. A local might quietly steer you to Modern, but the best plan is to try a couple and pick a side.

  • Modern Pastry Hanover St., North End · The locals' pick
  • Mike's Pastry Hanover St., North End · The famous one, and the photo
  • Bova's Bakery Salem St., North End · Late-night cannoli

A North End lunch legend

Arancini and Pizza at Galleria Umberto

You can't go to the North End without stopping at Galleria Umberto on Hanover Street. It's a no-frills lunch counter with a line out the door and one of our personal favorites.

Order an arancino, a golden fried rice ball stuffed with meat sauce, peas and cheese, and a few squares of their thick, Sicilian-style pizza. It's simple, cheap and absolutely delicious, the kind of food North Enders grew up on.

One warning: get there early. Umberto makes a set amount each day, and when it runs out, they close, usually in the early afternoon. Show up at 1:30 and you may find the doors locked.

  • Galleria Umberto Hanover St., North End · Lunch only; closes when sold out

Hot and buttered, or cold with mayo?

A Lobster Roll

Every New Englander has an opinion here. A Connecticut-style roll is warm lobster meat drenched in butter. A Maine-style roll is cold lobster tossed with a little mayo. Both come in a toasted, buttered split-top bun, and both are worth every penny.

Our top three: Neptune Oyster in the North End, whose hot buttered roll is legendary and worth the wait for a seat; Row 34 in Fort Point, a stylish oyster bar with a great roll either way; and Belle Isle Seafood in Winthrop, a no-frills seafood shack about ten minutes from Logan. It's the perfect first or last stop of your trip.

  • Neptune Oyster Salem St., North End · Get it hot with butter
  • Row 34 Fort Point · Oyster bar with a view of the scene
  • Belle Isle Seafood Winthrop, near Logan · Shack-style, huge portions

Order it like a local

North Shore Roast Beef, "Three-Way"

A "three-way" is thin-sliced rare roast beef with James River BBQ sauce, mayo and white American cheese on a grilled onion roll. Just say "large beef, three-way" and you'll sound like you grew up here.

The legend starts on Revere Beach. Kelly's opened in 1951 as a hot dog stand, and one night a wedding next door was canceled, leaving the owners with spare roast beefs. They sliced them thin and served them on grilled rolls the next day, and what was supposed to last the day sold out in an hour. Kelly's still claims to have invented the sandwich, and the North Shore has been arguing about who makes the best one ever since.

The one locals line up for is The Modern Butcher in Danvers, which serves its roast beef only on Saturdays. Lines run down the street, and when it's sold out, that's it for the week.

Our advice: you'll find a roast beef in Boston, but if you want the real thing, head north of the city. The easiest way is a trip to Revere Beach on the Blue Line. Grab a three-way at the original Kelly's on the boulevard, eat it on the seawall, and if you're here in late July, walk over to the International Sand Sculpting Festival.

  • Kelly's Roast Beef Revere Beach Blvd., Revere · The original, since 1951
  • The Modern Butcher Maple St., Danvers · Saturdays only

Say it with us: chowdah

New England Clam Chowder

Creamy, briny and full of clams and potatoes, real New England clam chowder is thick enough to stand a spoon in and comes with a pack of oyster crackers on the side. (Locals consider the tomato-based Manhattan version a mistake.)

For history with your bowl, sit at the oyster bar at Union Oyster House near Faneuil Hall, which has been serving since 1826 and is often called the oldest restaurant in America.

  • Union Oyster House Union St., near Faneuil Hall · Open since 1826

A North Shore summer ritual

Fried Whole-Belly Clams

Sweet, crunchy, a little messy and nothing like the clam strips you've had elsewhere. Whole-belly clams are the real thing, and the best are eaten at a picnic table near the water.

Woodman's of Essex, about an hour north, says it fried the first clam back in 1916. Combine it with a day trip to Salem or Cape Ann.

  • Woodman's of Essex Essex · Where the fried clam began

Duxbury, Wellfleet and beyond

Local Oysters

Cold New England water makes some of the best oysters in the world: crisp, salty and clean. Ask what's local that day and try a few side by side; oyster lovers can taste the difference between bays.

Raw bars are everywhere, from Neptune Oyster in the North End to the Seaport waterfront in summer.

  • Neptune Oyster North End
  • Row 34 Fort Point

It's actually a cake

Boston Cream Pie

Two layers of sponge cake, vanilla custard in the middle and chocolate glaze on top. It's the official dessert of Massachusetts, and yes, it's a cake.

It was invented at the Omni Parker House hotel in the 1850s, and you can still order the original in its restaurant, a block from Boston Common on the Freedom Trail.

  • Omni Parker House School St., downtown · Where it was invented

Old-school pies

North End and East Boston Pizza

Regina Pizzeria on Thacher Street has been making thin, chewy, slightly charred pies in the North End since 1926. Expect a line and a small, loud, wonderful dining room.

Santarpio's in East Boston, near the airport, is a local legend for its pizza and grilled sausage and lamb skewers. No frills, just great food.

  • Regina Pizzeria Thacher St., North End · Since 1926
  • Santarpio's Chelsea St., East Boston · Get the lamb skewers too

Best eaten under the Green Monster

A Fenway Frank

Is it the best hot dog in the world? Maybe not. Is it the best hot dog you'll eat at the oldest ballpark in the major leagues on a summer night? Absolutely. Add a sausage with peppers and onions from a cart on Lansdowne Street on the way in.

Speak the language

A Frappe, a Fluffernutter and a Dunkin'

In Boston, a milkshake with ice cream is a frappe. Order a "milkshake" and you may get just milk and syrup.

Marshmallow Fluff was invented in Somerville in 1917, and a Fluffernutter (Fluff and peanut butter on white bread) is a lunchbox classic for generations of New England kids.

And you will notice the Dunkin' on every corner. Locals drink iced coffee all year, even in a snowstorm. Order a "regular" and you'll get cream and sugar.

Why we're called Beantown

Boston Baked Beans (Sort Of)

Here's a local secret: nobody in Boston goes out for baked beans. The dish, navy beans slow-baked for hours with molasses and salt pork, is the reason Boston got the nickname "Beantown," back when the city was a hub of the molasses and rum trade.

You'll find them on a few historic menus and on souvenir shelves, and they're good. Just don't be surprised when the locals look at you funny if you ask where to get the best ones. Grab a box of Boston Baked Beans candy instead.
