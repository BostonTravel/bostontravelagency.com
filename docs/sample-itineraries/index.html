Sample Boston itineraries

Boston is compact, walkable and packed with history, which makes it perfect for a short trip. These sample itineraries are the plans we would build for our own friends and family: the essentials in two days, the city plus Cambridge and Fenway in three, and the full experience, with a day trip, in five.

Each day is paced for real people, with time to linger over lunch. Mix and match freely, and swap days around the weather and the season.

Day 1

Revolutionary Boston & the North End

Walk the Freedom Trail, eat your way through Little Italy and end with oysters at America's oldest restaurant.

Morning

Start at Boston Common and join a small-group Freedom Trail tour. In two and a half hours you will see the State House, the Granary Burying Ground, the Old State House, the Boston Massacre site and Faneuil Hall, with a guide who brings the 1770s to life.

Book this tourFreedom Trail History Small Group Walking TourCheck dates & prices →
Lunch

Follow the red line into the North End, Boston's Little Italy, for lunch.

  • Pizzeria Regina North End
    Brick-oven pizza on Thacher Street since 1926
  • Giacomo's North End
    Tiny, beloved and famous for its pasta. Expect a line.
Afternoon

Continue on the trail to the Paul Revere House and Old North Church, then walk across the bridge to Charlestown to board the USS Constitution. Take the ferry back downtown for a great view of the skyline. Pick up a cannoli on the way and choose your side in the Mike's vs. Modern debate.

  • Mike's Pastry or Modern Pastry Hanover Street
    The great cannoli rivalry
  • Caffe Vittoria Hanover Street
    Espresso in an old-world cafe
Evening

Dinner where Bostonians have eaten oysters for two centuries, or at the North End's favorite raw bar.

  • Union Oyster House Near Faneuil Hall
    America's oldest continuously operating restaurant, since 1826
  • Neptune Oyster North End
    Small, buzzy and known for its hot buttered lobster roll

Photo: LEvalyn, CC BY 4.0

Day 2

Beacon Hill, the Back Bay & the Harbor

Gas lamps and brownstones in the morning, a duck boat in the afternoon and a sunset cruise to finish.

Morning

Wander the cobblestone lanes of Beacon Hill (do not miss Acorn Street), then cross into the Public Garden to see the Make Way for Ducklings statues and, in season, ride the Swan Boats.

  • The Paramount Charles Street, Beacon Hill
    A neighborhood breakfast and brunch institution
  • Tatte Bakery Charles Street and around town
    Pastries and coffee in a bright, busy cafe
Midday

Hop on a duck boat for the city's most famous tour. It rolls past the landmarks, then drives straight into the Charles River.

Book this tourBoston Duck Boat Sightseeing TourCheck dates & prices →
Afternoon

Stroll Newbury Street's shops and cafes, then see Copley Square: Trinity Church and the Boston Public Library, whose courtyard is one of the city's hidden gems. For a bird's-eye view, head up to View Boston at the top of the Prudential Center.

  • Saltie Girl Back Bay
    Seafood and an enormous tinned-fish menu
  • Atlantic Fish Co. Boylston Street
    A classic Back Bay seafood room
Evening

Take a harbor sunset cruise from the waterfront, then have dinner in the Seaport.

Book this tourBoston Harbor Sunset CruiseCheck dates & prices →
  • Row 34 Fort Point, Seaport
    Oysters and local beer in a converted warehouse
  • Legal Harborside Seaport
    Three floors of seafood with harbor views

Photo: Seasider53, CC BY 4.0

Day 3

Cambridge & Fenway

America's oldest university in the morning and America's oldest ballpark in the afternoon.

Morning

Ride the Red Line to Harvard Square and take a student-led tour of Harvard Yard, then browse the bookstores and cafes around the square. If you have time, walk down Massachusetts Avenue to see MIT.

Book this tourHarvard University Campus Guided Walking TourCheck dates & prices →
  • Alden & Harlow Harvard Square
    Creative small plates in a basement dining room
Afternoon

Head back across the river to Fenway Park. The tour takes you onto the field level and up on top of the Green Monster.

Book this tourTour of Historic Fenway ParkCheck dates & prices →
Evening

In season, stay for a Red Sox game. Otherwise, catch the Celtics or Bruins at TD Garden, or grab a burger near the park.

  • Bleacher Bar Lansdowne Street
    A bar built into Fenway's outfield wall, with a window onto the field
  • Tasty Burger Fenway
    A casual local burger favorite

Day 4

A Taste of Boston

A food tour, a tea party and a night out in the South End, the city's best dining neighborhood.

Morning

Join a food and history tour that combines tastings with the stories of the North End, the Freedom Trail and Beacon Hill.

Book this tourFood and History: North End, Freedom Trail and Beacon HillCheck dates & prices →
Afternoon

Throw tea into the harbor yourself at the Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum, then wander the Rose Kennedy Greenway or browse the stalls of the Boston Public Market.

Book this tourBoston Tea Party Ships & MuseumCheck dates & prices →
Evening

Have dinner in the South End, Boston's restaurant row, or try dim sum and dumplings in Chinatown. Finish with a Boston cream pie where it was invented.

  • Toro South End
    Lively Spanish tapas
  • Myers + Chang South End
    Playful Asian small plates
  • Gourmet Dumpling House Chinatown
    Soup dumplings and scallion pancakes
  • Omni Parker House Downtown
    Birthplace of the Boston cream pie and Parker House rolls

Photo: Tim Sackton, CC BY-SA 2.0

Day 5

Day Trip: Salem, Concord or Plymouth

Get out of the city for the day. Choose witches and sea captains, the shot heard round the world, or the Pilgrims.

Option 1: Salem

Take the ferry to Salem and explore the witch trials story, the Peabody Essex Museum and the House of the Seven Gables. October is magical, and very busy.

Book this tourSalem High-Speed Ferry from BostonCheck dates & prices →
  • Turner's Seafood at Lyceum Hall Salem
    Seafood in a historic hall where Alexander Graham Bell once lectured
Option 2: Lexington & Concord

Stand on Lexington Green and Concord's Old North Bridge, where the Revolution began on April 19, 1775, then visit Walden Pond and Louisa May Alcott's Orchard House.

Book this tourPrivate Day Trip to Lexington and ConcordCheck dates & prices →
Option 3: Plymouth

Board Mayflower II, visit Plimoth Patuxet Museums and see Plymouth Rock, with a stop in Quincy, home of two presidents, on the way.

Back in Boston, toast your trip with a classic Italian dinner in the North End.

  • Mamma Maria North Square, North End
