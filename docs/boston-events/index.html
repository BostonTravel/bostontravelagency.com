Plan around the big days

Boston events calendar

Boston's calendar is packed with traditions, from the Marathon on Patriots' Day to the feasts of the North End and First Night on New Year's Eve. Here are the events we think are worth planning a trip around, listed in order of what's coming up next.

Where organizers have announced dates, we show them. Otherwise we show the date the event usually falls on, so check the official site before you book flights or hotels.

Oct·

Salem Haunted Happenings

All of October
Salem · Typical timing

In October, the whole city of Salem dresses up: costume parades, candlelit ghost tours, haunted houses, psychics and street performers fill the historic downtown.

This is where the 1692 witch trials took place, and Salem tells that story with care at its memorials and museums. Add the colonial seaport, the House of the Seven Gables and fall foliage, and there is no better place to spend a day in October.

Make it part of your trip: skip the traffic and parking by taking the ferry from Long Wharf, about 50 minutes across the harbor. Go on a weekday if you can; weekends near Halloween are very busy.

Dec3

Boston Common Tree Lighting

Likely Thu, Dec 3, 2026
Boston Common · Typical timing: Usually the first Thursday in December

Carolers, ice skating on the Frog Pond and thousands of twinkling lights draped through Boston Common, all leading up to the countdown to light the city's official Christmas tree.

Every year since 1971, Nova Scotia has sent Boston its tree as a thank-you for the city's help after the 1917 Halifax Explosion. It is a warm holiday tradition with a story behind it.

Make it part of your trip: stroll Beacon Hill's gaslit streets afterward, and see the holiday windows and the tree at Faneuil Hall.

Dec16

Boston Tea Party Reenactment

Likely Wed, Dec 16, 2026
Old South Meeting House and the waterfront · Typical timing: On or around December 16

Join the crowd at Old South Meeting House as colonists in costume argue over the tax on tea, then follow the 'Sons of Liberty' to the waterfront and watch the tea go into the harbor.

On December 16, 1773, this protest helped spark the American Revolution. The reenactment happens where it really started, in the same meeting house where Samuel Adams spoke, and audiences are invited to shout along.

Make it part of your trip: visit the Boston Tea Party Ships and Museum, where you can toss tea overboard yourself, and walk the Freedom Trail the same week.

Dec31

First Night Boston

Thu, Dec 31, 2026
City Hall Plaza, Copley Square and the waterfront · Annual date

A parade, glowing ice sculptures, live music on stages across the city and two rounds of fireworks, the last at midnight over Boston Harbor.

Boston started First Night in 1976, and the idea of a citywide, family-friendly New Year's Eve arts celebration has since spread around the world. It is free, and the T is free in the evening, so the whole city comes out.

Make it part of your trip: see the ice sculptures by daylight, catch the early fireworks over Boston Common with kids, and head to the waterfront for midnight. Dress warmly.

Feb·

Lunar New Year in Chinatown

Late January or February, following the lunar calendar
Chinatown · Typical timing

Drums boom, cymbals crash and firecrackers pop as bright red and gold lions dance their way down Beach Street, stopping at every shop and restaurant to 'eat' the lettuce hung in the doorways for good luck.

Boston's Chinatown is one of the oldest in the country, and the Lion Dance Parade is its biggest day of the year. It is loud, joyful and completely free, and kids love following the lions from door to door.

Make it part of your trip: come hungry. Dim sum on Lunar New Year morning is a Boston tradition, and Chinatown is a short walk from the Theater District and Boston Common.

Mar14

St. Patrick's Day Parade

Likely Sun, Mar 14, 2027
South Boston · Typical timing: The Sunday closest to March 17

Bagpipes, marching bands, floats and a sea of green fill Broadway in South Boston, with hundreds of thousands of people cheering along the route.

Few American cities are as proudly Irish as Boston, and the parade also marks Evacuation Day, when George Washington's army drove the British out of Boston on March 17, 1776. It is history and a party rolled into one.

Make it part of your trip: wear green, take the Red Line to Broadway, and finish the day in one of the city's historic Irish pubs.

Apr·

Red Sox Opening Day at Fenway

Late March or early April; see the Red Sox schedule
Fenway Park · Typical timing

Red, white and blue bunting hangs from Fenway's grandstands, the whole team lines up along the baselines, and the first pitch at Opening Day feels like the end of a long New England winter.

Fenway Park opened in 1912 and is the oldest ballpark in the major leagues. Seeing the Green Monster on Opening Day, with the city buzzing all around Kenmore Square, is something Red Sox fans never forget.

Make it part of your trip: tickets are tough, so if you miss out, a Fenway Park tour gets you up on the Green Monster any day of the season.

Apr19

Patriots' Day Reenactments

Mon, Apr 19, 2027
Lexington and Concord · Annual date

Before sunrise, hundreds of people gather on Lexington Green to watch minutemen in tricorn hats face down a column of redcoats, just as their ancestors did on April 19, 1775.

This is the very spot where the American Revolution began, and the reenactors are passionate about getting every detail right. Standing in the dawn chill as the first musket shots ring out gives you chills of a different kind.

Make it part of your trip: pair it with the Freedom Trail in Boston, then drive the Battle Road to Concord's Old North Bridge for the afternoon ceremonies.

Apr19

Boston Marathon

Mon, Apr 19, 2027
Hopkinton to Boylston Street · Annual date

Half a million people line the 26.2 miles from Hopkinton to Boylston Street, ringing cowbells, handing out orange slices and cheering for perfect strangers by name.

Run every year since 1897, Boston is the world's oldest annual marathon and the one serious runners dream of qualifying for. The roar from the crowd as runners turn onto Boylston for the final stretch is pure Boston.

Make it part of your trip: watch near the finish line in the Back Bay or at Heartbreak Hill in Newton, and book your hotel months ahead. Marathon Monday is also Patriots' Day, so you can catch the dawn reenactment first.

May28

Boston Calling Music Festival

Likely May 28 to 30, 2027
Harvard Athletic Complex, Allston · Typical timing: Memorial Day weekend, Friday to Sunday

Three days of headline bands, local favorites, food trucks and summer sunshine on the fields just across the river from Harvard Square.

Boston Calling kicks off the summer for the whole city, and it is easy to reach by T, so you can stay downtown and still catch every set.

Make it part of your trip: spend the mornings in Cambridge exploring Harvard Yard and Harvard Square, then head to the festival for the afternoon and evening shows.

Jul4

Fourth of July and the Boston Pops Fireworks

Sun, Jul 4, 2027
Charles River Esplanade · Annual date

Hundreds of thousands of people spread blankets on the Esplanade as the Boston Pops play patriotic favorites at the Hatch Shell, finishing with the 1812 Overture, real cannon fire and fireworks bursting over the Charles.

In July 1776 the Declaration of Independence was read to Bostonians from the balcony of the Old State House, and the reading is repeated there every Fourth. The whole city celebrates like it. On the morning of the Fourth, USS Constitution sails out for its annual turnaround cruise in the harbor.

Make it part of your trip: hear the Declaration read from the Old State House balcony in the morning, then claim a spot on the Esplanade by early afternoon. A harbor cruise is a great way to see the city from the water that week.

Jul23

International Sand Sculpting Festival

Jul 23 to 25, 2027 Dates confirmed
Revere Beach · Confirmed

World-class sand sculptors from around the globe turn America's first public beach into an open-air gallery, carving castles, faces and fantastic creatures that seem impossible to make from sand and water alone.

It is one of the biggest free events in New England, with fireworks, food vendors, rides and live music along the boardwalk. Watching the artists work, grain by grain, over the three days is half the fun.

Make it part of your trip: take the Blue Line straight to Revere Beach station in about 20 minutes from downtown, and stay for a swim and a famous Kelly's roast beef sandwich.

Aug12

Fisherman's Feast

Likely Aug 12 to 15, 2027
North End · Typical timing: Mid-August, Thursday to Sunday

Strings of lights, Italian music, sizzling sausage and peppers and fresh zeppole fill the narrow streets of the North End for four days in August.

Started in 1910 by fishermen from Sciacca, Sicily, this is Boston's oldest Italian feast. The highlight is the Flight of the Angel on Sunday, when a young girl dressed as an angel is lowered on ropes above North Street to honor the Madonna. Crowds hold their breath.

Make it part of your trip: it is steps from the Paul Revere House and Old North Church, so combine it with a North End food tour or the Freedom Trail.

Aug27

Saint Anthony's Feast

Likely Aug 27 to 29, 2027
North End · Typical timing: The last full weekend of August

The 'Feast of all Feasts' takes over Endicott and Thacher Streets with food stalls, cannoli, strolling singers and a festival crowd that feels like a giant neighborhood block party.

On Sunday, the statue of Saint Anthony is carried through the North End for about ten hours, covered in ribbons of dollar bills pinned on by the faithful and followed by marching bands. It is a living piece of Italian-American history you will not find anywhere else.

Make it part of your trip: go on Sunday for the procession, then grab a cannoli on Hanover Street. The whole neighborhood looks forward to this weekend all year.

Oct15

Head of the Charles Regatta

Likely Oct 15 to 17, 2027
Charles River · Typical timing: The weekend of the third Sunday in October

Thousands of rowing crews race up the winding Charles River, three at a time, while spectators line the bridges and riverbanks with coffee, cider donuts and blankets.

It is the largest three-day rowing event in the world, drawing Olympians, college crews and masters rowers from around the globe, all against a backdrop of fall leaves, Harvard's boathouses and the Boston skyline.

Make it part of your trip: watch from the Weeks or Eliot Bridges for the best views of the tight turns, and book your hotel early. October is Boston's busiest season.

Planning tips

  • Hotels sell out and prices jump on Marathon weekend, Head of the Charles weekend and college graduation weekends in May. Book as early as you can.
  • For July 4th and First Night, take the T; streets near the Esplanade and the waterfront close to traffic.
  • The North End feasts are free. Go hungry and bring cash for the street vendors.
